Each document carries the date of its last change. This page says what changed, newest first. A change that is unfavourable to you, or that widens what we collect, is also announced by email to active accounts, as the [terms](/legal/terms/) and the [privacy policy](/legal/privacy/) provide.

## 25 September 2026

- **Terms of service**: the platform can no longer give access to a machine on its own. It relays your devices' keys and the approvals they sign, and the agent adds a key only if it already trusts it or a device it trusts approved it; it never removes the last key. The heartbeat carries the fingerprints of the keys the agent trusts and of those waiting for an approval.
- **Privacy**: the key approvals your devices sign, and those fingerprints in what we receive from your machines.
- **Data processing**: key approvals and those fingerprints within the scope and the categories of data.

## 24 September 2026

- **New documents**: the [subscription terms](/legal/billing/), which will apply the day billing opens; the [cookie policy](/legal/cookies/); the list of [sub-processors](/legal/sub-processors/); the [security policy](/legal/security/) and its `security.txt` file; the [third-party software](/legal/third-party/); the [legal notice](/legal/legal-notice/); and this history.
- **Terms of service**: encrypted backups to your own storage — what the platform receives about them, and what it never receives, neither the backup, nor its passphrase, nor the access key to your storage; the agent version, sent with each heartbeat as well; the capacity to contract; the pointers to the subscription terms and to the legal notice.
- **Privacy**: backup references; the basis of each processing; the answer to the audience measurement banner, the only value the measurement writes to your browser, and the link to change your mind; the fonts served by Google Fonts; the visit count of an affiliate link; the right to restrict a processing and to withdraw a consent; the CNDP and the rights of California residents.
- **Data processing**: backup references within the scope, the backups themselves outside it, and the details of the processing.
- **Licence**: what the law lets you do in spite of the ban on decompiling, and the pointer to the third-party software.
- **Acceptable use**: infringement of the rights of others and how to report it to us, vulnerability research under the security policy, and backups in what we can see.
