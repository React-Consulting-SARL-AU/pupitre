## The service

Pupitre is a desktop app, an agent installed on a machine you own — a VPS, a dedicated server, a computer — and a console. The app inspects your machine, installs the services you choose, hardens it, and becomes your window onto it: projects, terminals, coding agents, databases, remote editors. The console holds your account, your organisation, the list of your machines and your subscription.

An account is required. Everything belongs to an organisation: a personal one is created with your account, and you may create or join others.

## What Pupitre receives from your machine

The agent on your machine only ever makes outbound connections: to `app.pupitre.studio`, and, when you turn backups on, to the S3 storage you chose. It reads its state every thirty seconds and sends a heartbeat every five minutes. Here is everything the platform receives, and nothing else:

- at enrolment: the name and address you gave the machine in the app, the public half of its SSH host key, the agent version and the processor architecture;
- with each heartbeat: the size and the free space of the disk, the size and the use of the memory, the load average, the version of the installed stack, the names of the installed catalogue modules, the name of the account that holds the SSH keys, and the names of the running tmux sessions — which are usually the names of your projects; when backups are on, the backup interval and the date and error of the last attempt;
- for each backup, when backups are on: its place in your storage (endpoint, bucket and object prefix), the digest of its manifest, its size, how many databases, projects and folders it holds, the configuration revision, the agent version, and the public key it is encrypted to — never a name, a file, or anything that opens it;
- from the app, not from the machine: the public halves of your devices' SSH keys, so that the agent can authorise them.

The platform never receives a file, a line of code, an environment variable, a secret, a log, the content of a terminal or of a database, the output of a command, or anything that lives inside your projects. It has no technical means of asking for them: the protocol between the agent and the platform has no such message. A backup goes from your machine to your storage, encrypted to a key only your passphrase gives, without passing through the platform; neither the app, nor the machine, nor Pupitre keeps that passphrase.

## What Pupitre can do on your machine, and what it cannot

The platform has exactly three levers on your machine, all of them applied by the agent itself when it reads its state:

1. **The right of use.** The platform tells the agent whether your organisation's subscription is valid. Without a valid answer for seven days, or when the answer is a suspension, the agent enters restricted mode: it refuses commands from the app and nothing else. It stops no service, deletes no data and touches no file.
2. **The list of authorised keys.** The platform gives the agent the public keys of the devices of the members of your organisation who are assigned to that machine. The agent writes them into a tagged block of the `authorized_keys` file of the SSH account it manages, atomically, without touching any line outside that block. Revoking a device or removing a member removes its key on the next read. A suspended machine receives an empty list: the keys of your Pupitre devices leave that block, and nothing else changes — keys you added yourself outside the block, and any other access you keep to the machine, stay as they are.
3. **The version to run.** The platform names the agent version to install. The agent downloads it from the platform and verifies its Ed25519 signature against the key it carries before installing it. A binary that does not carry that signature is never installed.

Everything else is impossible by construction, not by policy:

- the platform cannot open a connection to your machine: nothing listens for it, it holds no address it could reach, no credential and no key that would open a session;
- it cannot run a command, read, write or delete a file, start or stop a service, project or process;
- there is no hidden access, no support access and no maintenance access. When support needs to see something on your machine, it asks you to send it;
- the app on your computer talks to your machine directly, over SSH, with your own key. The platform is not on that path and nothing that passes between the app and the machine goes through Pupitre. When a project is exposed on a domain, the tunnel is on your own Cloudflare account, mounted from your computer, and the platform is not on that path either.

What the agent does on your machine — installing, configuring, starting, stopping — it does on the orders of your app, on your computer, sent over your SSH session. Nothing happens on the machine without a gesture of yours, except what the agent does on its own for the three levers above and for the services it monitors.
