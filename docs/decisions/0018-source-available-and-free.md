# 0018 — Source available, free up to three servers, licence beyond

Date: 2026-10-01 · Status: accepted · Replaces [0001](./0001-closed-source.md); amends [0007](./0007-stripe-managed-payments.md) and [0009](./0009-entitlement-bound-to-server.md)

**The code becomes public.** The repository is published under the **Apache 2.0 licence with the Commons Clause** ([`LICENSE`](../../LICENSE), [`NOTICE`](../../NOTICE)). The licensor is **React Consulting SARL AU** (Morocco), which also publishes the platform and pays for its hosting. Anyone can read, modify, build and self-host Pupitre, including a platform of their own. No one can sell it: the Commons Clause removes the right to provide, for a fee, a product or service whose value derives, entirely or substantially, from Pupitre — a host that charges for installing Pupitre in one click, a paid "managed Pupitre", a resold version of the app. The name and the logo are not granted.

This is **not open source** in the OSI sense, and we never say so: in French "code source disponible" or "code source ouvert à la lecture", in English *source-available*.

**Why Apache 2.0 + Commons Clause.** A well-known permissive licence, with its patent clause and its contribution regime (section 5), rather than a home-made licence; the Commons Clause, a short public text, removes from it the one thing to protect — resale — with no copyleft that would hinder enterprise use. The trust we ask of the customer — a root binary on their server — is better earned with code they can read than with hidden code.

**Free up to three servers.** Each organization enrols `FREE_SERVERS = 3` servers with no card, no trial, no subscription, no time limit (`@pupitre/shared/plans`). Beyond that, a **licence** is required. The existing seat system is kept and renamed "licence" everywhere it is visible: an organization's quota is `FREE_SERVERS` plus the seats of its live subscription (`granted` product or Stripe). The platform's organization keeps `PLATFORM_ORGANIZATION_SEATS = 100`. An enrolment beyond the quota is refused with `seat_quota_reached`, with the message: free up to three servers, a licence beyond, `support@pupitre.studio`.

**An organization's licence**, as the platform judges it: `valid` with a live subscription, or without a subscription as long as its counted servers fit within `FREE_SERVERS`; `grace` when the subscription is unpaid (the previous seven-day grace period); `suspended` when the platform has suspended, closed or is deleting the organization, or when it holds more servers than its quota without a live licence — a granted licence that expires, for example —, after the same grace period. The platform's organization is always `valid`. The agent keeps its states (`valid`, `grace`, `restricted`, `dev`) and the seven-day grace period of [0009](./0009-entitlement-bound-to-server.md).

**The licence system is dormant.** Today, the only way to exceed three servers is for a platform admin to grant a `granted` licence (the granting gesture that already existed). The Stripe code stays in place but is unused: `BILLING_MODES = ["stripe", "off"]`, production runs on `off` — checkout and portal refused, nothing calls Stripe. The day licences are sold, we switch to `stripe`; [0007](./0007-stripe-managed-payments.md) then says how, except that the product sold is a licence beyond the free servers and no price is set.

**What disappears.** The thirty-day trial, the launch mode and its "kept for good" seat (`LAUNCH_*`, `launch.ts`, the workflows that aligned it), the Solo, Team and Hosted offers and their prices, the months offered by an affiliate link. Affiliate links remain, for **tracking only**: clicks per day, sign-ups, and servers enrolled by the organizations they brought in.

**The `entitlement` → `license` rename**, down into the protocol: `PROTOCOL_VERSION = 3`, `hello.license`, `snapshot.license`, `license` in the result of `enroll`, the `license_required` refusal, a generation `{ protocol: 3, app: "2.0.0", agent: "2.0.0" }` in the compatibility sheet. `/me` and `/agent/state` return `license` and keep `entitlement` (and `subscription: null` for `/me`) for apps and agents older than 2.0.0. The agent's cache goes from `/var/lib/pupitre/entitlement.json` to `license.json` through registry migration 8. On the customer side, "Abonnement requis" becomes "Licence requise".

**What does not change.** The rules of [`architecture.md`](../architecture.md): the customer's server is the source of truth, no private key outside the laptop, no inbound connection, the app usable for seven days without the platform, nothing readable deposited on the server — a binary and configuration files. The binary is no longer obfuscated (garble removed): security never rested on keeping the code secret, and [`security.md`](../security.md) now says so.

Consequences: everything committed is public — secrets, customer data and internal notes never enter the repository, gitleaks already checks this; an outside contribution arrives by pull request to `staging`; the legal pages are rewritten around the licence and React Consulting SARL AU, whose identifiers `LEGAL_ENTITY` carries. No email announces this change: as of 1 October 2026, the only active account is the owner's.
